01Cardboard. An idea. A Rafale.

I made this model entirely on my own, without any help. The material was reclaimed cardboard; my starting point was a photograph of the Rafale, from which I took the measurements needed for the build.

The project connects my interest in aviation with a physical build. I had to give the aircraft a shape using a simple material, while keeping the complete model in mind as I worked on each part.

02From a photograph to a volume.

I built the wings, cockpit, nose and landing gear. Turning an image into an object calls for attention to proportions, structure and the position of each element within the silhouette.

More information

The photograph was my reference for the measurements. The questions were straightforward: how could I reproduce the aircraft’s shapes, make the different parts fit together and keep a coherent structure? The wings define much of the silhouette, the nose extends the fuselage, the cockpit gives detail to the front and the landing gear adds another element to build. Working on these parts required patience and care.

The illustration on this page shows a reference silhouette of the Rafale, with its delta wing and forward canards. It is neither a photograph nor a measured plan of my model.

03Building, and learning.

This build taught me patience, care in my work and the importance of structure. It also helped me learn to manage a project independently and deepen my knowledge of aviation.

More information

Building without help means keeping sight of the whole while working through the details. The model led me to connect the measurements taken from the photograph with the aircraft’s different parts, then consider their place in the complete structure. Reclaimed cardboard provided an accessible starting point: the value of the project came from the construction work, attention to shape and persistence needed to make progress.
